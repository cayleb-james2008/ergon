Motion storyboard

The pack's behaviour layer for the DSH-era faces: four budgets, one ease-out curve, and what is forbidden. Source of record: design/motion-spec.md in the pack; adopted from the Duet motion budget (ruling 20261031T171200Z). These are the floor, not a ceiling; existing Windows surfaces keep their documented 180 ms view transition.

Applies to: tap feedback (buttons, tabs, list selection) · content replacement (tab/view swap, list → detail) · content arriving (message, card, live update, brief) · sheets and dialogs.

One ease-out curve

One curve across the product, defined once at build time — no per-component easing inventions. The illustration shows the shape only; the build records the exact cubic-bezier with the motion tokens.

Applied examples: a tab switch swaps at 200 ms · a new message enters at 250 ms · the approval sheet opens at 350 ms · a tap acknowledges at 120 ms. If removing the motion loses no meaning, it is not admitted.

Forbidden (the unslop fence)

No loops

Pulse, ping, breathing glows, shimmer sweeps, marquees, auto-advancing carousels.

No spectacle

Glow, parallax, 3D tilt, decorative blur, confetti, magnetic cursors, particle effects.

No shortcuts

No transition-all; animate the named property only. Animation never blocks input.

No motion-only meaning

Every animated state change has a static, worded equivalent (state disc + word).

Reduced motion and High Contrast

  • Decorative motion is off under Windows High Contrast and under reduced-motion settings; state changes remain immediate and legible.
  • High Contrast wins over the pack: system colours replace tokens, and nothing relies on animation to communicate.
  • Focus rings are never animated (pack rule: 2 px accent ring, always visible on keyboard focus).
