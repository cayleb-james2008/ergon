## Glossary (each term defined once, here, before its first use)

- **Nox, the owner, the Lead, a lane, the executing session.** Nox rules on designs. The owner is Cayleb. The Lead is the fleet's coordinating agent; a lane is any other agent session in the fleet. The executing session is the Claude session that wrote this pack and builds the plugin.
- **Duet.** The owner's Claude agent in the Claude mobile app, running in Anthropic's cloud. Since your 07:57 ET owner rule (`125700Z`) it is the fleet's only Claude door.
- **Bus, handoff, facade, participant, token, scope.** The bus is the fleet's message layer. A handoff is a message to a participant. The facade is its front door, an MCP server with 15 tools, six of them `bus.*`. A participant has an id and a kind (`duet` is both an id and a kind). A token is the credential its holder presents; a scope is the list of calls that token may make. A call outside the scope answers `scope-denied`.
- **Pull participant; wake; "delivered into a pull".** A pull participant (Duet, Nox) is never woken by the bus; it reads its mail when it chooses to. For fleet lanes "wake" means a followup injected into a live agent. For Duet we claim a wake only for what a probe shows. "Delivered into a pull" means the handoff sits where Duet reads on his own next check.
- **Courier, relay session, rendezvous, stub, bell, variants D and S.** The courier (`duet-courier`) is a small sandboxed user service that pulls Duet's mail from the bus as `duet`, writes it as a file into a shared Google Drive folder ("FleetBus", the rendezvous), polls for the answer and posts it back. The stub is an empty `ack-<id>.txt` file Duet's answer is written into. The bell is Google's share-notice mail, which may prompt Duet's next check. The relay session (`claude:duet-relay`) is a Claude session that would post into Duet's chat instead. Variant D: a dedicated second Google account owns the folder. Variant S: the owner's own account does.
- **Exposure classes E0 to E3** (design section 2): E0 the owner carries messages by hand; E1 outbound-only to a store Duet already reads, or a relay session; E2 an owner-hosted relay with a custom connector; E3 publish a service. **Name clash:** "E1" is also the id of your 11:55 ET ruling on relayed elevation approval. This pack writes "class E1" for the exposure class and "ruling E1" for the approval reading.
- **Case W / case C.** In case W any program running as the owner, a lane included, can use the owner's control socket (so it can act as the owner). ## 1. What you are ruling on, and what it unblocks

You are ruling on whether the Part A design is the route to build: the fleet reaches Duet through a Google Drive folder that an outbound-only courier writes and polls, with a relay session (`claude:duet-relay`) as the preferred route only if it turns out a session can post into Duet's chat. The design recommends exposure class E1: nothing of ours is published to the internet, and no new party holds plaintext. A `duet` bus participant carries the traffic. Its token is held by the delivery process and never by Duet, and it can only send a closed `reply`. Authority comes from the authenticated channel and is never read from message content. The design admits plainly that nothing can wake Duet except possibly a relay session, and that nobody has shown that works.

Your Part A ruling and the exposure recommendation to the owner both gate on this pack. A yes unblocks, in order: G40 (read first), the offline comms amendments (C1 to C5), the courier and fake-Duet harness, the Lane 6 G1 gate, and only then the owner acts and the single proof ping. The owner declared (your filing `20261102T203243Z`) that once Duet's connection is proven he shuts down the Claude desktop app; and the mission order's done-when is "Duet's wake-and-ack proof through the bridge, reported to Nox with evidence". So this leg is on the critical path, and it is also the one where the platform's limits (pickup in hours, not seconds) matter most.

**If you have ten minutes, rule these first:** PA-10 (does waiting on L4-A, your ruling 4, stand for `duet`), PA-4a (the relay session's posting method), PA-7 (G40 first, who runs it, a wrong path to fix), PA-13 (the proof ping's bytes), PA-14 (which Lane 6 conditions you adopt, and the gate's address), PA-9 (build the comms amendments, C4 included).
